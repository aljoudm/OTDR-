# Validation experiments: results

Generated by `scripts/run_experiments.m`. Default parameters: L = 2 km, Tw = 100 ns, f_rep = 10 kHz, events at 1200 m (200 Hz, 1 rad) and 1500 m (500 Hz, 0.5 rad), 2001 pulses. In experiment 1 both events have 1 rad.
## 1. Spatial resolution (dz = c*Tw/(2n) = 10.2 m)

Two events of 1 rad (200 Hz and 500 Hz). The response is the part of the fibre where the detected intensity changes over time more than twice the noise floor; separate responses are separated by at least 3 samples (about 3 m) at the noise floor.

| Event spacing | Separate responses | Extent of each response (m) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 m | 1 | 20.4 |
| 20 m | 2 | [15.3 15.3] |

Figure: `figures/experiments/exp1_resolution.png`. The response of one event is about as long as the spatial resolution plus the disturbed section. Events 5 m apart (below the resolution) merge into one response; events 20 m apart are resolved into two.

## 2. Pulse width

| Tw (ns) | Resolution (m) | Peak (nA) | Noise floor (nA) | Peak/floor | Extent above 2x floor (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 5.1 | 5.1 | 1.00 | 5.1 | 7.1 |
| 100 | 10.2 | 14.5 | 1.00 | 14.5 | 15.3 |
| 200 | 20.4 | 35.9 | 1.00 | 35.9 | 27.6 |

Figure: `figures/experiments/exp2_pulse_width.png`. A longer pulse widens the response (worse resolution) and raises the detected signal (better SNR).

## 3. Frequency limit (Nyquist)

f_v(1) = 6000 Hz, f_rep = 10 kHz -> expected alias |6000 - 10000| = 4000 Hz. Detected frequency: **3998 Hz**. The unambiguous limit is f_rep/2 = 5 kHz (f_rep <= c/(2nL) = 51 kHz for 2 km).

Figure: `figures/experiments/exp3_aliasing.png`.

## 4. OTDR vs Phi-OTDR with the same vibration

- Conventional OTDR: largest difference between the traces with and without vibration = 0 (relative 0): **unaffected**.
- Phi-OTDR: relative difference of the detected intensity = 0.12: **the vibration is detected**.

Figure: `figures/experiments/exp4_otdr_vs_phiotdr.png`.

## 5. Coherent fading (different fibres)

The fibre (random scatterers) changes with the seed, so the event lands on a different speckle value. The phase error is the circular standard deviation of the recovered phase against the injected 200 Hz, 1 rad waveform. The noisy receiver adds 200 nA of noise per I and Q component to the two COTDR samples (additive at the detector output, equivalent to a larger sigma_n).

| Seed | Detected position (m) | Weakest amplitude / median | Phase error, normal receiver (rad) | Phase error, noisy receiver (rad) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1209.0 | 1.56 | 0.001 | 0.12 |
| 2 | 1208.0 | 1.79 | 0.010 | 0.11 |
| 3 | 1212.1 | 1.03 | 0.001 | 0.17 |
| 4 | 1208.0 | 0.72 | 0.007 | 0.22 |
| 5 | 1211.0 | 0.84 | 0.001 | 0.18 |
| 6 | 1205.9 | 1.08 | 0.106 | 0.20 |
| 7 | 1213.1 | 0.60 | 0.001 | 0.27 |
| 8 | 1205.9 | 0.34 | 0.013 | 0.53 |
| 9 | 1211.0 | 0.40 | 0.002 | 0.40 |
| 10 | 1207.0 | 0.98 | 0.005 | 0.16 |
| 11 | 1209.0 | 1.86 | 0.000 | 0.10 |
| 12 | 1207.0 | 0.54 | 0.108 | 0.32 |

With the normal receiver (1 nA) every fibre gives a clean waveform (error below 0.11 rad), even when the weakest amplitude is only a third of the median. With the noisy receiver the best fibre is seed 11 (error 0.10 rad) and the worst is seed 8 (error 0.53 rad): when the event lands on a fading point (small amplitude at k1 or k2) the recovered phase becomes noisy, a live example of coherent fading (Shang, Section 3.2). Shifting k1 and k2 by a few samples in `das_postprocess.m` moves them away from the fading point.

Figure: `figures/experiments/exp5_fading.png`.
